# CS-255 System Analysis and Design

This repository contains coursework and portfolio artifacts from **CS-255: System Analysis and Design** at Southern New Hampshire University (SNHU).

## Course Overview

CS-255 focuses on the principles, methods, and techniques used to analyze business requirements and design information systems. Throughout the course, I worked with the **DriverPass** scenario to identify client needs, develop system requirements, and create system design models using Unified Modeling Language (UML).

## DriverPass Project

DriverPass is a proposed system designed to help students prepare for their driver's license exams by providing online practice tests, training resources, and the ability to schedule on-the-road driving instruction.

The project involved analyzing the client's business requirements and translating those requirements into a system design that could be understood by both technical and nontechnical stakeholders.

## Repository Contents

This repository contains coursework from each module as well as the two primary portfolio artifacts from the DriverPass project:

* **Project One – Business Requirements Document:** Demonstrates my ability to gather, analyze, and document client requirements.
* **Project Two – System Design Document:** Demonstrates my ability to translate business requirements into a technical system design using UML diagrams and system specifications.

These artifacts demonstrate my understanding of:

* Business and system requirements
* Functional and nonfunctional requirements
* System analysis and design
* UML diagrams and process modeling
* Communicating technical system designs to different stakeholders

## Project Reflection

### Briefly summarize the DriverPass project. Who was the client? What type of system did they want you to design?

The client for this project was DriverPass, a company that wanted to provide students with better preparation for their driving tests. DriverPass wanted an online system that would allow customers to create accounts, purchase training packages, access online practice tests and training materials, and schedule driving lessons. The system also needed to support different user roles and allow employees and administrators to manage the information and functions appropriate to their responsibilities. My role was to analyze the client's business requirements and translate them into a system design that could guide the development of the DriverPass system.

### What did you do particularly well?

I think one of the strongest parts of my work was translating the client's requirements into organized system documentation and UML diagrams. Creating use case, activity, sequence, and class diagrams allowed me to examine the same system from several different perspectives. I was able to show what users needed the system to do, how users would interact with it, how processes would flow through the system, and how different components would relate to one another. I also worked to keep the design focused on the requirements provided by DriverPass rather than adding unnecessary functionality.

### If you could choose one part of your work on these documents to revise, what would you pick? How would you improve it?

If I could revise one part of my work, I would spend additional time refining the UML diagrams. Although the diagrams communicate the major processes and relationships within DriverPass, I would review them again from the perspective of a developer who had never seen the project before. I would look for areas where relationships, decision points, labels, or system interactions could be made even clearer. My goal would be to make the documentation detailed enough that another developer could understand the intended system behavior without needing me to explain the design.

### How did you interpret the user's needs and implement them into your system design? Why is it so important to consider the user's needs when designing?

I interpreted the user's needs by first identifying the functions DriverPass expected the system to perform and then translating those requirements into specific system features and UML models. Requirements involving customer accounts, scheduling driving lessons, purchasing packages, accessing training materials, and managing information were represented throughout the system design. Considering user needs is important because a system can work correctly from a technical standpoint and still fail if it does not solve the user's actual problem. Keeping the user's needs at the center of the design helps ensure that the finished system is useful, understandable, and aligned with the client's business objectives.

### How do you approach designing software? What techniques or strategies would you use in the future to analyze and design a system?

My approach to software design is to understand the problem and requirements before deciding how the system should be built. I would begin by gathering requirements from stakeholders and separating functional requirements from technical, security, and performance requirements. I would then use tools such as use cases and UML diagrams to model the system from different perspectives before implementation begins.

My professional experience in automation has also taught me the importance of thinking about troubleshooting and maintenance during the initial design instead of waiting until problems occur. In future projects, I would continue using an iterative approach where requirements and designs are reviewed, tested against the user's needs, and refined before development. I would also place a strong emphasis on clear documentation so future developers and maintainers can understand why the system was designed the way it was.
